addition

The water supply enters procurement — a bidder, a price and a December target, and still no water balance

1 September 2026 · v1.81 · assessment v2.42

On 28 August 2026 the BCDA Board granted original proponent status to a consortium of Maynilad Water Services Inc. and the Korea Water Resources Corporation (K-water), the operator of Angat Dam, for an unsolicited proposal reported at ₱14.8 billion to supply water and manage wastewater at New Clark City. It must still clear the public–private partnership process and a Swiss challenge, and BCDA’s December 2026 award date is a conditional target. This is the first concrete procurement step toward the water this report calls the binding constraint, and the first thing to say about it is what it is not: original proponent status is not a contract award, not a construction commitment, and not evidence that the water exists. Three BCDA figures arrived with it, all attributed and none verified. New Clark City’s existing capacity is an estimated 20–30 million litres a day. Estate capacity could rise to as much as 150 MLD, with the initial system designed for up to 135 MLD. And contemporaneous reporting of the grant repeated BCDA’s earlier estimate that Pax Silica could eventually require 65–90 MLD. Two ratios follow: this one project wants between 2.2 and 4.5 times everything New Clark City can supply today, and roughly half to two-thirds of the initial designed capacity of a system that must also serve every other user in the estate. Those are offered as scale and not as an allocation — nothing establishes that the 135 MLD would be available here, and the system is an estate-wide utility rather than a Pax Silica project. What the procurement step does not supply is the thing the water section has asked for throughout. As at 1 September 2026 no water balance, catchment delineation, dry-season yield analysis, flow-duration curve, dependable drought yield, withdrawal inventory or environmental-flow reservation for this system was located in publicly available BCDA, Maynilad, K-water, NWRB or DENR-EMB material, and the announcement of the grant identifies no selected catchment. We state that as a publication finding rather than a claim that no such work exists — an unsolicited proposal accepted to this stage presumably rests on technical work, and the point is that none of it is public at the moment the process moves. A December award on a proposal whose water balance has never been published would place the contract ahead of the evidence. The catchment arithmetic does not change and neither does the conclusion it supports. Separately, the social-screening section records the launch in Quezon City on 31 August 2026 of the Network Opposed to Pax Silica, the first named national multi-sectoral coalition against the project, at which Petronila Capiz-Muñoz, identified as chieftain of the Aeta community at Sitio Sapang Kawayan, said the community had not been consulted and had again been told to leave, this time by December. That is attributed testimony; its weight is that it carries in the chieftain’s own voice, at a public event, the account this report has held since 18 August from two other residents. It establishes no official deadline, and displacement remains disputed. The adversarial gate ran one pass over five numbered claims and refuted one of them: the drafter had written that BCDA restated the 65–90 MLD requirement, and the gate held that the BCDA-derived announcement does not itself restate it, so the published wording is the gate’s own. It also struck a categorical claim that no hydrological analysis exists in favour of the dated publication finding above, forbade any implication that the 135 MLD would be available to this project, forbade describing the system as rainwater-only, forbade adopting a concession term while February reporting says 50 years and Maynilad’s August disclosure says 25, and required the chieftain’s account to be carried strictly as testimony. Asked directly whether any of this justifies moving a key judgment, confidence level, scenario weight, precondition or red line, it answered no. Nothing moves. One figure was adjudicated at rail level and is recorded here because the record should show it: the Manila Bulletin reported the project as ₱5 billion in its headline and ₱4.8 billion in its body, against ₱14.8 billion in BusinessMirror, the Philippine News Agency and the roughly ₱15 billion reported when the proposal first surfaced in February. The gate held ₱14.8 billion safe to carry; only that figure is used.

The timeline gains 10–11 September 2026: the inaugural Luzon Economic Corridor Investment Forum in Manila, co-hosted by the governments of the United States, the Philippines and Japan and sponsored by the US Trade and Development Agency, convening a stated 600 investors, industry leaders and senior officials. What those pages do not do is mention Pax Silica, semiconductors, New Clark City or the proposed economic security zone, and the new row says so on its face. The executive order was signed on 18 August, not 21 August — and the discrepancy is recorded rather than explained away

28 August 2026 · v1.79 · assessment v2.40

The order's execution clause reads: DONE, in the City of Manila, this 18th day of August. frequently asked questions

Direct answers, condensed from the assessment. Every figure is traceable to the report sections on this page.

What is Pax Silica?

Pax Silica is a United States-led initiative — a programme of the US State Department, not an international organisation — coordinating what it calls trusted supply chains for semiconductors, AI infrastructure, critical minerals and energy. Its founding Declaration is deliberately non-binding: it creates no obligations, no financing and no enforceable benefits. Pax Silica has 25 signatories; Taiwan endorses its principles without signing.

What is proposed for New Clark City?

The Philippines has publicly offered approximately 1,618.74 hectares of state land — 17.1% of New Clark City, in Capas and Bamban, Tarlac — to host the initiative's first "Economic Security Zone": a semiconductor, AI-infrastructure and advanced-manufacturing district with a named but uncommitted anchor tenant. No survey, technical description or map annex of the parcel has been published.

Is anything signed or legally binding yet?

No. What exists is a non-binding declaration signed on 16 April 2026, a four-page BCDA proposal letter, a named but uncommitted anchor tenant, and roughly thirty unnamed letters of interest. Nothing about the project is legally binding, financed, contracted or permitted at the project level, and fourteen of sixteen implementation gates are open.

Is this assessment for or against the project?

Neither. The assessment endorses nothing and rejects nothing. Its recommendation is that the Philippines should decline to sign in November 2026 unless seven defined preconditions — disclosures and binding conditions, all within the executive's control — are satisfied first. None is met as at the latest verification.

What are the seven preconditions?

(1) The parcel legally defined and Annex A published; (2) the draft framework published with a public comment period; (3) independent water-balance and grid-adequacy studies published; (4) Year-3 rent and the lease term determined against an independent land valuation; (5) DFA-OLA and OSG legal opinions published; (6) the NCIP certification precondition process formally initiated; (7) the activity mix and underlying economic studies published. If any one is unmet in November, the assessment's recommendation is not to sign.

What are the biggest unresolved issues?

Water and power are the binding physical constraints: harvesting the stated 90 MLD from rainfall would need a catchment of between 90% and 193% of the entire site, and the 3 GW power figure is about a fifth of Luzon's peak demand, on a grid that ran a 373 MW deficit in May 2026. Separately, no published model reconciles US$40–70 billion of investment with 130,000–190,000 direct jobs.

Why does November 2026 matter?

Both governments hope to sign a framework agreement during the ASEAN Summit in November 2026. The assessment's decision rule: a genuinely non-binding framework that expressly creates no rights leaves the signature as a photograph; any instrument that grants land, fixes terms or creates entitlements before the seven preconditions are met crosses the red line — substantive rights before due diligence is complete.
