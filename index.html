The arithmetic is a screening test, not a design verdict. At 90 million litres a day the catchment range is 90–193% of the parcel before competing land uses and seasonal storage; sufficiency has not been demonstrated. At 65 million, favourable assumptions reduce the required area but do not establish a workable design. Her preferred configuration is a stand-alone system with its own generation, renewable or gas. On 9 August BCDA said proponents are studying a dedicated power plant so the facility would not jeopardise residential supply — directionally consistent with the regulator’s own-generation requirement, though a study establishes neither a stand-alone configuration nor capacity. The interview puts the load at about 19% of Luzon’s projected 2026 peak, matching this report’s figure by an independent route, and notes it is close to the entire Visayas grid. The 1,300 MW is a paper shortfall, not a gap between committed and required capacity: it is what is left after setting 500 MW of ACWA solar nameplate and the 1,200 MW LNG option, still at feasibility-study stage, against the 3,000 MW required. Neither figure is firm committed capacity, and a 500 MW solar plant does not produce 500 MW at night, so the firm shortfall on the record is larger. The LNG option carries a logistics problem of its own: it arrives by ship, but Clark has an airport, not a seaport, so the gas must land at a coastal terminal and reach the plant by an inland delivery route that no published document specifies. The unsolicited Subic–Clark pipeline proposal is for jet fuel, a liquid fuel, and nothing on the record identifies it as that route. Set against BCDA’s Q1 2028 site-development target, none of these timelines fit. What changes here is not the direction of the finding but its standing: the department that would have to approve grid connection has said publicly that it will not approve it at scale.

Benchmark: BCDA's own signed ACWA Power lease, 500 hectares for up to 500 MW of solar plus storage, i.e. 1 ha per MW of nameplate. Solar at 20% capacity factor. The difference is not technical. If the United States ultimately received a 99-year master lease over the 1,618.74 hectares — no published contractual instrument establishes the final structure, term, counterparty or legal basis — the arrangement could, depending on the lease terms, applicable Philippine law and required BCDA and other governmental approvals, permit subleasing to locators or the use of leasehold rights as financing security. That would concentrate a materially different degree of long-term leasehold control in a foreign state than a structure in which BCDA grants separate leases directly to individual locators, which would be an agency-to-locator leasing structure of the kind used in economic-zone development; any such rights would remain subject to the lease's conditions and remaining term, and it would not transfer Philippine ownership or sovereignty. The published project-specific contractual documents address only layer 1 and do not settle which of the two is intended. In April, BCDA's president was reported describing the US as master lessee — developing the hub's infrastructure and utilities while BCDA retains ownership (Manila Bulletin, 24 April 2026) — an attributed official description, not a contractual commitment; later BCDA remarks referred more broadly to foreign-investor leases of up to 99 years. On 12 August, Sen. frequently asked questions

Direct answers, condensed from the assessment. Every figure is traceable to the report sections on this page.

What is Pax Silica?

Pax Silica is a United States-led initiative — a programme of the US State Department, not an international organisation — coordinating what it calls trusted supply chains for semiconductors, AI infrastructure, critical minerals and energy. Its founding Declaration is deliberately non-binding: it creates no obligations, no financing and no enforceable benefits. Pax Silica has 25 signatories; Taiwan endorses its principles without signing.

What is proposed for New Clark City?

The Philippines has publicly offered approximately 1,618.74 hectares of BCDA-owned land — 17.1% of New Clark City, in Capas and Bamban, Tarlac — to host the initiative's first "Economic Security Zone": a semiconductor, AI-infrastructure and advanced-manufacturing district with a named but uncommitted anchor tenant. No survey, technical description or map annex of the parcel has been published.

Is anything signed or legally binding yet?

The Declaration was signed on 16 April 2026 but is non-binding. The reviewed record identifies no binding commitment to implement the proposed zone, zone financing, procurement or project-specific permit; fourteen of sixteen implementation gates remain open. The separate signed MCC energy threshold grant is not identified as zone financing, and its agreement text remains unreviewed.

Is this assessment for or against the project?

Neither. The assessment endorses nothing and rejects nothing. It recommends declining any instrument that creates substantive rights unless all seven preconditions are satisfied and the red lines are respected. A strictly non-binding framework may be signed only with every express safeguard in the two-branch rule at §23.3. None of the seven preconditions is recorded as met; a November target does not waive them.

What are the seven preconditions?

(1) The parcel legally defined and Annex A published; (2) the draft framework published with a public comment period; (3) independent water-balance and grid-adequacy studies published; (4) Year-3 rent and the lease term determined against an independent land valuation; (5) DFA-OLA and OSG legal opinions published; (6) the NCIP certification precondition process formally initiated; (7) the activity mix and underlying economic studies published. If any one is unmet in November, the assessment's recommendation is not to sign an instrument creating substantive rights; a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only with every express safeguard in the two-branch rule at §23.3.

What are the biggest unresolved issues?

Water and power are the binding physical constraints: harvesting the stated 90 MLD from rainfall would need a catchment of between 90% and 193% of the entire site, and the 3 GW power figure is about a fifth of Luzon's peak demand, on a grid that ran a 373 MW deficit in May 2026. Separately, no published model reconciles US$40–70 billion of investment with 130,000–190,000 direct jobs.

Why does November 2026 matter?

Philippine officials hope BCDA and an unidentified US counterpart will sign a contemplated framework or master agreement in November 2026, but the target remains subject to negotiation and possible delay. The assessment's two-branch decision rule: a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only if its text expressly creates no lease or land right, confers no investor entitlement or exclusivity, commits no public funding or incentives, leaves every project subject to ordinary Philippine law, permits and approvals, permits either government to withdraw, and subjects later binding instruments to separate approval and public disclosure — a framework silent on those points is treated as binding; any instrument that grants land, fixes terms or creates entitlements before the seven preconditions are met crosses the red line — substantive rights before due diligence is complete.
