What is Pax Silica?

Pax Silica is a United States-led initiative — a programme of the US State Department, not an international organisation — coordinating what it calls trusted supply chains for semiconductors, AI infrastructure, critical minerals and energy. Its founding Declaration is deliberately non-binding: it creates no obligations, no financing and no enforceable benefits. Pax Silica has 26 signatories (as of 23 October 2026); Taiwan endorses its principles without signing.

What is proposed for New Clark City?

The Philippines has publicly offered approximately 1,618.74 hectares of BCDA-owned land — 17.1% of New Clark City, in Capas and Bamban, Tarlac — to host the initiative's first "Economic Security Zone": a semiconductor, AI-infrastructure and advanced-manufacturing district with a named but uncommitted anchor tenant. No survey, technical description or map annex of the parcel has been published.

Is anything signed or legally binding yet?

The Declaration was signed on 16 April 2026 but is non-binding. The reviewed record identifies no binding commitment to implement the proposed zone, zone financing, procurement or project-specific permit; fourteen of sixteen implementation gates remain open. The separate signed MCC energy threshold grant is not identified as zone financing, and its agreement text remains unreviewed.

Is this assessment for or against the project?

Neither. The assessment endorses nothing and rejects nothing. It recommends declining any instrument that creates substantive rights unless all seven preconditions are satisfied and the red lines are respected. A strictly non-binding framework may be signed only with every express safeguard in the two-branch rule at §23.3. None of the seven preconditions is recorded as met; a signing target does not waive them.

What are the seven preconditions?

(1) The parcel legally defined and Annex A published; (2) the draft framework published with a public comment period; (3) independent water-balance and grid-adequacy studies published; (4) Year-3 rent and the lease term determined against an independent land valuation; (5) DFA-OLA and OSG legal opinions published; (6) the NCIP certification precondition process formally initiated; (7) the activity mix and underlying economic studies published. If any one is unmet at signature, the assessment's recommendation is not to sign an instrument creating substantive rights; a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only with every express safeguard in the two-branch rule at §23.3.

What are the biggest unresolved issues?

Water and power are the binding physical constraints: harvesting the stated 90 MLD from rainfall would need a catchment of between 90% and 193% of the entire site, and the 3 GW power figure is about a fifth of Luzon's peak demand, on a grid that ran a 373 MW deficit in May 2026. Separately, no published model reconciles US$40–70 billion of investment with 130,000–190,000 direct jobs.

Why does November 2026 matter?

Philippine officials previously identified November 2026 as a possible signing target for a contemplated framework or master agreement involving BCDA and an unnamed US counterpart. In a reply relayed by the Palace on 13 October, BCDA said partnership details remained under discussion and signing was expected before the end of 2026, without reaffirming November or the ASEAN Summit. On 19 October Manila Bulletin reported BCDA’s president saying the agreement remains targeted for November; November is an attributed target, not a confirmed date. The assessment's two-branch decision rule: a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only if its text expressly creates no lease or land right, confers no investor entitlement or exclusivity, commits no public funding or incentives, leaves every project subject to ordinary Philippine law, permits and approvals, permits either government to withdraw, and subjects later binding instruments to separate approval and public disclosure — a framework silent on those points is treated as binding; any instrument that grants land, fixes terms or creates entitlements before the seven preconditions are met crosses the red line — substantive rights before due diligence is complete.
