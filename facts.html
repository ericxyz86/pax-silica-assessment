Pax Silica in the Philippines: key facts

Published by Agile Intelligence, under human editorial direction. Download the full report (PDF) or read the living assessment.

What this page is. A one-page fact sheet. It restates, word for word, the answers, findings and key figures that the independent assessment publishes on its homepage, and it is regenerated whenever the site publishes. It adds nothing of its own; if the two ever differ, the live assessment prevails.

What is Pax Silica?

Pax Silica is a United States-led initiative — a programme of the US State Department, not an international organisation — coordinating what it calls trusted supply chains for semiconductors, AI infrastructure, critical minerals and energy. Its founding Declaration is deliberately non-binding: it creates no obligations, no financing and no enforceable benefits. Pax Silica has 26 signatories (as of 23 October 2026); Taiwan endorses its principles without signing.

What is proposed for New Clark City?

The Philippines has publicly offered approximately 1,618.74 hectares of BCDA-owned land — 17.1% of New Clark City, in Capas and Bamban, Tarlac — to host the initiative's first "Economic Security Zone": a semiconductor, AI-infrastructure and advanced-manufacturing district with a named but uncommitted anchor tenant. No survey, technical description or map annex of the parcel has been published.

What are the six findings?

What each finding says, in the assessment’s own words.

01 — the instrument does not describe the project

The published BCDA offer proposes a bilateral coordination office and states that the property "shall be used exclusively" for five coordination functions. It contains no reference to semiconductor packaging, data centres, mineral processing, or private locators. The public prospectus has no published legal basis.

02 — the headline figures do not reconcile

Pairing US$40–70bn with 130,000–190,000 direct jobs is unreconciled and jointly implausible: constructible on paper, but the one illustrative mix this report constructed needs more back-end employment in this one site than the whole national industry supports today, and no published mix reconciles them. The claimed PHP 68–75bn of annual withholding tax, which BCDA has confirmed is payroll withholding, implies average pay of PHP 2.13m at an assumed 20% effective rate and 160,000 jobs, about 8.2 times the annualised PSA average wage for time-rated full-time workers in formal establishments with 10 or more workers, a general formal-sector benchmark, not semiconductor pay. The named anchor is expected to generate "thousands."

03 — water and power are unsolved, not solved

Harvesting 90 MLD from rainfall needs a catchment of 1,460–3,129 hectares, or 90% to 193% of the site. 3 GW is 19.2% of the DOE’s 15,600 MW projection for 2026 Luzon peak demand and 20.6% of the 14,534 MW actually recorded by mid-2026, on a grid that ran a 373 MW deficit under red alert in May 2026. The DFC is funding an LNG feasibility study, not a solar one.

04 — the counterparty relationship is asymmetric

On 24 July 2026 a US 12.5% forced-labour tariff on Philippine goods took effect. Narrower than the headline: semiconductors and other Section 232 articles are exempt, and it replaced an expiring 10% measure, so the net increment is about +2.5 points. The direction is what matters. The Philippines is being asked to grant land and rent forgone, for a project that would also require substantial water and electricity whose sources, allocation, price, infrastructure and cost bearer remain unresolved, in exchange for a non-binding declaration and feasibility studies.

05 — provider-labelled sampled net sentiment was most negative in july, and on water, land and governance

Under the listening provider’s labels, net sentiment among 6,437 mentions in its stated 10% sample was −1.0 before the Philippines’ accession (December to 15 April) and −24.8 after it (16 April to 27 July). July, at −34.8, was the most negative month in the record. Among the eight reported, overlapping keyword-matched topic categories, net sentiment was near zero for AI and technology (−1.5), geopolitics and security (0.0), jobs and economy (+2.6), and minerals and mining (+2.3). The three lowest values were governance and corruption (−54.4), land and indigenous (−50.3), and water and environment (−43.8). This card previously highlighted the report’s classifier-labelled historical series. Following a 17 September 2026 audit, the report withdrew its explanation of that series’ positive early-month levels and its use of the classifier-labelled April–May decline as evidence against the provider-labelled May recovery. The audit did not examine the historical mentions. These are machine-labelled sampled mentions, not a measure of the Philippine public’s views. 06 — the strategic case is genuine, and some criticism is wrong

Comparative non-entanglement with Chinese capital is a real and time-limited asset. BPO at 7.4% of GDP faces AI displacement. And some of the most widely circulated environmental critiques do not survive scrutiny: silicon rather than nickel is the semiconductor input, and the light-pollution claim, while a legitimate and mitigable screening item, is substantially overstated as popularly framed.

Is anything signed or legally binding yet?

The Declaration was signed on 16 April 2026 but is non-binding. The reviewed record identifies no binding commitment to implement the proposed zone, zone financing, procurement or project-specific permit; fourteen of sixteen implementation gates remain open. The separate signed MCC energy threshold grant is not identified as zone financing, and its agreement text remains unreviewed.

Is this assessment for or against the project?

Neither. The assessment endorses nothing and rejects nothing. It recommends declining any instrument that creates substantive rights unless all seven preconditions are satisfied and the red lines are respected. A strictly non-binding framework may be signed only with every express safeguard in the two-branch rule at §23.3. None of the seven preconditions is recorded as met; a signing target does not waive them.

What are the seven preconditions?

(1) The parcel legally defined and Annex A published; (2) the draft framework published with a public comment period; (3) independent water-balance and grid-adequacy studies published; (4) Year-3 rent and the lease term determined against an independent land valuation; (5) DFA-OLA and OSG legal opinions published; (6) the NCIP certification precondition process formally initiated; (7) the activity mix and underlying economic studies published. If any one is unmet at signature, the assessment's recommendation is not to sign an instrument creating substantive rights; a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only with every express safeguard in the two-branch rule at §23.3.

What are the biggest unresolved issues?

Water and power are the binding physical constraints: harvesting the stated 90 MLD from rainfall would need a catchment of between 90% and 193% of the entire site, and the 3 GW power figure is about a fifth of Luzon's peak demand, on a grid that ran a 373 MW deficit in May 2026. Separately, no published model reconciles US$40–70 billion of investment with 130,000–190,000 direct jobs.

Why does November 2026 matter?

Philippine officials previously identified November 2026 as a possible signing target for a contemplated framework or master agreement involving BCDA and an unnamed US counterpart. In a reply relayed by the Palace on 13 October, BCDA said partnership details remained under discussion and signing was expected before the end of 2026, without reaffirming November or the ASEAN Summit. November was the previously reported target, reiterated in Manila Bulletin’s 19 October account; when asked about signing that month on 3 November, BCDA’s president did not reaffirm it and said nothing was final. On 4 November, Palace Press Officer Claire Castro said she was relaying BCDA’s message that a US response to the draft was awaited; asked about a summit signing, she said “Let us see”. On 6 November, Ambassador Romualdez said he did not think the framework would be ready by the time President Trump visits, and that finalisation was hoped for before the end of the year but was “most likely maybe early next year”. On 7 November, US Ambassador Lee Lipton, addressing media comments about an announcement on the zone during or after the summit, said no such announcement “was ever planned” and that decisions would be made “at the appropriate time”; he set no decision or signing date and did not mention a signing. Reports of Trade Undersecretary and BOI Managing Head Ceferino Rodolfo’s 10 November remarks quote him saying there would be no announcement during President Trump’s meeting with President Marcos and that any signing would have a separate occasion. Rappler quotes him saying the project should have “its own dedicated moment” and “I think we’re still on track towards having an agreement by the end of this year”. Asked what remained to be finalised, he answered, according to BusinessMirror, “The master agreement itself, the whole thing”. The reports identify no signing date or venue. No signing date is confirmed. The assessment's two-branch decision rule: a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only if its text expressly creates no lease or land right, confers no investor entitlement or exclusivity, commits no public funding or incentives, leaves every project subject to ordinary Philippine law, permits and approvals, permits either government to withdraw, and subjects later binding instruments to separate approval and public disclosure — a framework silent on those points is treated as binding; any instrument that grants land, fixes terms or creates entitlements before the seven preconditions are met crosses the red line — substantive rights before due diligence is complete.
