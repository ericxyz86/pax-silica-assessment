Signing readiness: the countdown, and the distance to the checklists

The countdown

The anchor, as the assessment's claim register records it: BCDA targets signing of the framework agreement by November 2026, potentially during the ASEAN Summit. The register grades this an official target, and records that on 19 August 2026 two same-day press reports — not independent of each other, and with no primary transcript located — rendered the BCDA president's description of the target as "not set in stone" and "not cast in stone." No source located by this assessment names a day within November, so the countdown runs to both bounds of the target month.

Comparison with the §23.3 decision rule

The assessment's decision rule (§23.3) has two branches. For any binding instrument: all seven preconditions must first be satisfied and every red line respected in the text. As of 10 September 2026, none of the seven is met, and no draft text had been located in the published record, so red-line compliance could not be evaluated against a text available to the assessment. For a strictly non-binding instrument: under the rule, it is consistent with the assessment's conditions only if its text expressly creates no lease, no land right, no entitlement and no funding commitment. The precondition test of the binding branch can already be applied to the record — as of 10 September 2026, 0 of 7 are met. The text-dependent matters — an instrument's binding character, express non-binding terms, and red-line compliance — cannot be evaluated unless and until a proposed text is located or otherwise made available for review.

The assessment's own stated recommendation: "The recommendation is neither endorsement nor rejection. It is that the Philippines should decline to sign in November 2026 unless a defined set of disclosures and binding conditions are satisfied first." And of the preconditions themselves, in the assessment's own words: none "takes more than a few months," all "are within the executive's control," and "the only cost of insisting on them is a delayed photograph."
