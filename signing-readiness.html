The countdown

The anchor, as the assessment’s claim register records it: BCDA targets signing of the framework agreement by November 2026, potentially during the ASEAN Summit. The register grades this an “Official target, publicly described by the BCDA president as subject to negotiation and possible delay”, and records the qualifications. On 19 August 2026 BusinessWorld and GMA News reported BCDA President Joshua M. Bingcang as saying BCDA was still aiming for November but that negotiations could take longer. The two outlets rendered his description of the target as “not set in stone” and “not cast in stone” respectively. They carry the same remarks by the same official on the same day, so they do not corroborate each other independently, and no primary transcript or recording was located. Reports of 14–15 September 2026 of continued government optimism about a November signature come from a single press encounter with Trade Undersecretary Ceferino Rodolfo. The register records them as not independent and without a located transcript, and states that they do not turn the target into a confirmed signing date. No source in the assessment record as of 1 October 2026 names a day within November, so the counters below run to both ends of the month.

Comparison with the §23.3 decision rule

The rule’s first branch applies to an instrument that creates any of the substantive rights it enumerates. The assessment states: “The Philippines should not sign any instrument that grants land possession or exclusive use; fixes a long lease term; leaves rent undetermined; restricts Philippine jurisdiction or regulatory authority; grants any immunity; commits public infrastructure or fiscal incentives; creates mineral-supply obligations; permits subleasing, transfer or collateralisation of any land right; or gives any investor or foreign government preferred rights — unless all seven preconditions at §23.1 have first been satisfied and the red lines at §23.2 are respected in the text.” The precondition test can be applied to the record now: as of 1 October 2026, 0 of 7 are met. Neither the red-line test nor the question of whether the instrument grants any of the enumerated rights can be applied. No text, draft or term sheet of the contemplated instrument was located in the assessment record as of 1 October 2026, so neither can be evaluated.

The second branch covers a strictly non-binding framework. Such a framework is acceptable under the rule “only if the text itself expressly states that it creates no lease or land right; confers no investor entitlement or exclusivity; commits no public funding or incentives; leaves every project subject to ordinary Philippine law, permits and approvals; permits either government to withdraw; and subjects every subsequent binding commercial instrument to separate approval and public disclosure. A framework that is silent on these points should be treated as branch one.” The reported descriptions of a master agreement are not a text. The assessment records that they bear on the branch-one classification if a lease or other land right were granted, without settling it. The text-dependent questions are therefore open: what rights, if any, the instrument creates and for whom, whether it contains the express non-binding terms, and whether it respects the red lines. They cannot be evaluated until a proposed text is located. The question the assessment says should be asked of any tabled text is: “what rights, if any, does this text create, and for whom?”

The assessment’s own stated recommendation: “The recommendation is neither endorsement nor rejection. The Philippines should not sign an instrument creating substantive rights before the required conditions are met; a strictly non-binding framework is acceptable only with every express safeguard of §23.3.” Of a non-binding signature, it adds: “If the answer is ‘none, expressly,’ the signature is legally a photograph, although political lock-in may still follow (KJ-16).”
